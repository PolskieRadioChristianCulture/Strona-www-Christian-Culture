5,800+ New Testament Manuscripts — Why the Bible Is Historically Reliable

Compared to every other work of classical antiquity, the New Testament stands unmatched in manuscript quantity, geographic spread, and proximity to the original events.

Skeptics often claim that the Bible has been altered beyond recognition over the centuries. Yet textual criticism tells the exact opposite story: over 5,800 Greek manuscripts—plus more than 10,000 Latin and thousands of early Syriac, Coptic, and Aramaic copies—allow scholars to verify the text with extraordinary precision.

Fragments such as P52 (John 18) date within decades of the original composition, whereas most classical works (Caesar, Tacitus, Plato) survive in only a handful of copies written 800 to 1,400 years after the author.

This unmatched manuscript preservation means that when you read Scripture today, you are reading the authentic apostolic testimony.
