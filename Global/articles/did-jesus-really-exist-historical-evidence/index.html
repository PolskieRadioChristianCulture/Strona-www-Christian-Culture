Did Jesus Really Exist? Historical Evidence Outside the Bible

Roman historians, Jewish chroniclers, and early manuscripts form a remarkably solid historical record confirming the life and crucifixion of Jesus of Nazareth.

Few figures in antiquity are attested by as many independent, near-contemporary sources as Jesus of Nazareth. Beyond the four Gospels, major non-Christian historians of the first and second centuries recorded His life, execution under Pontius Pilate, and the rapid rise of His followers.

Cornelius Tacitus (Annals 15.44) explicitly notes that Christus suffered the extreme penalty under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. Flavius Josephus (Antiquities 18 & 20), Pliny the Younger, Suetonius, and the Babylonian Talmud all corroborate key historical anchors.

Far from being a late myth, the historical Jesus stands at the center of documented Roman and Jewish history—inviting every honest seeker to examine not only His existence, but His message of grace and resurrection.
